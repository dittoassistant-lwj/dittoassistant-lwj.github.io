CIERA: Cross-Iteration Exponent Reuse for Lossless Allgather in Sharded MoE Training

Authors & affiliations: Ali Zafar Sadiq and Haiying Shen — University of Virginia; Masahiro Tanaka — Anyscale.

Tags: MoE Training, Collectives/Allgather, Lossless Compression

Why this is the top pick: This is the strongest network-facing AI-systems signal today: it attacks a concrete collective bottleneck in sharded MoE training without changing model numerics.

Deeper explanation: CIERA is interesting because it does not treat communication compression as a stateless codec bolted onto a collective. It exploits a training-time invariant: exponent fields in MoE expert weights are often stable across adjacent iterations after warmup. By caching those exponents at receivers, sending sign and mantissa on unchanged entries, and only enabling the path for shards with positive net benefit, the method turns Allgather into a stateful, selective, overlapped protocol. For Ethan’s world, the architectural lesson is sharp: collective libraries for superpods should expose enough tensor/runtime state to make fabric decisions based on temporal stability and critical-path savings, while preserving exact numerics when convergence risk is unacceptable.

  • Problem: Sharded data-parallel MoE training must Allgather expert weight shards before layers execute. That communication can dominate iteration time, while common compression approaches either lose numerical fidelity or ignore stability across iterations.
  • Key contributions:
  • Introduces Cross-Iteration Exponent Reuse Allgather: cache stable floating-point exponents locally and transmit sign plus mantissa when exponents are unchanged.
  • Adds benefit-driven selective compression so only parameter shards with net time savings are compressed, rather than applying a one-size-fits-all transform.
  • Overlaps compression/decompression with Allgather and adjacent computation; reports bitwise-exact reconstruction with 3.70× speedup over a lossless baseline on OLMoE-1B-7B at 16 GPUs, with trace-driven projections to 128 GPUs.
  • Why it matters architecturally: Stateful metadata and selective compression can reduce collective bottlenecks without asking model owners to accept lossy training behavior.
  • Useful diagram: Figure 6 is the useful architecture diagram: it shows CIERA’s exponent-reuse compression path, benefit-driven shard selection, and computation–communication pipelining around Allgather.

CIERA: Cross-Iteration Exponent Reuse for Lossless Allgather in Sharded MoE Training

Authors & affiliations: Ali Zafar Sadiq and Haiying Shen — University of Virginia; Masahiro Tanaka — Anyscale.

Tags: MoE Training, Collectives/Allgather, Lossless Compression

Problem: Sharded data-parallel MoE training must Allgather expert weight shards before layers execute. That communication can dominate iteration time, while common compression approaches either lose numerical fidelity or ignore stability across iterations.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Introduces Cross-Iteration Exponent Reuse Allgather: cache stable floating-point exponents locally and transmit sign plus mantissa when exponents are unchanged.
  • Adds benefit-driven selective compression so only parameter shards with net time savings are compressed, rather than applying a one-size-fits-all transform.
  • Overlaps compression/decompression with Allgather and adjacent computation; reports bitwise-exact reconstruction with 3.70× speedup over a lossless baseline on OLMoE-1B-7B at 16 GPUs, with trace-driven projections to 128 GPUs.

Why it matters: This is the strongest network-facing AI-systems signal today: it attacks a concrete collective bottleneck in sharded MoE training without changing model numerics.

Key insights:

  • The compression opportunity comes from optimizer dynamics: after warmup, many weight exponents do not change between adjacent iterations, even though mantissas keep moving.
  • Lossless matters for training infrastructure because it avoids the “did communication compression perturb convergence?” argument that usually follows lossy collectives.
  • Selective compression is the right systems move: small or low-benefit shards should bypass the path if compression cost exceeds saved network time.
  • The design turns an Allgather into a stateful protocol with cached receiver-side metadata, which is closer to transport/session design than a pure tensor codec.

Actionable lessons: For MoE fabrics, track temporal stability in tensors, not just tensor size. A collective library could expose per-shard benefit estimates, cached metadata state, and overlap windows so compression is applied only when it reduces critical-path time.

Adaptive Context Parallelism for Production LLM Serving

Authors & affiliations: Jiarui Guo, Rongle Wang, Peijun Huang, Zongwei Lv, Xiaolin Wang, and Tong Yang — Peking University; Ziqing Wang, Kan Liu, Tao Lan, and Lin Qu — Alibaba Group.

Tags: LLM Serving, Context Parallelism, Prefix Cache

Problem: Long-context serving makes prefill compute and KV/cache placement highly uneven. Static context-parallel degrees waste GPU time on short requests and under-serve long requests, while naive dynamic choices can destroy prefix-cache locality.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Presents Vertumnus, a production-oriented adaptive context-parallel serving system for heterogeneous, temporally changing workloads.
  • Routes requests using a placement cost that combines predicted queuing delay, cache-aware prefill time, and GPU-time cost across workers with different CP degrees.
  • Adds seconds-scale worker split/merge operations and global prefix-cache management; reports up to 28.1% lower mean TTFT and 13.3 percentage-point higher token-weighted SLO attainment on a 64-GPU cluster.

Why it matters: It is a clean example of serving control-plane design where parallelism degree, queueing, and cache locality are coupled rather than tuned independently.

Key insights:

  • Context parallelism is not only a kernel-level optimization; in production it becomes a fleet-composition problem because demand for long-context workers changes over time.
  • Prefix-cache locality is a first-class scheduling input. A request may be cheaper on a busier worker if its prefix state is already there.
  • The paper’s split/merge operations are a useful abstraction for elastic serving pools: resize CP worker groups without rethinking the entire deployment.
  • For networked/disaggregated KV designs, the same placement-cost structure should include remote-cache transfer cost and fabric pressure.

Actionable lessons: Do not schedule long-context serving by request length alone. Use a cost model that prices queueing, prefill, GPU-time, and cache movement together; otherwise the serving tier may burn network/cache locality to save a small amount of compute.

verified arXiv paper · optical data-center networks, wavelength borrowing, traffic engineering

A Wavelength Borrowing Architecture for Optical Data Center Networks - Extended Version

Authors & affiliations: Andrea Detti, Chiara Lodovisi, and Silvello Betti — CNIT, University of Rome “Tor Vergata”, Electronic Engineering Department, Italy.

Tags: Optical DCN, Topology Engineering, Traffic Engineering

Problem: Spine-leaf fabrics see unbalanced east-west demand. A fully static optical layer strands capacity under skew, while full reconfigurability can add component cost and operational complexity.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Proposes a spine-leaf all-optical architecture where lightly used wavelengths can be borrowed by leaf nodes with higher demand.
  • Combines wavelength borrowing with two-hop detouring to control wavelength load while limiting detoured traffic volume.
  • Introduces a tunable borrowing degree B and shows near-optimal behavior can be reached below full reconfigurability, using data-center-grade AWGs, AWGRs, colorless OXCs, and combiners.

Why it matters: For AI clusters, this is relevant as a topology-engineering lever for persistent skew: MoE phases, storage bursts, and pod-to-pod training traffic often create demand matrices that are not all-to-all uniform.

Key insights:

  • The key knob is partial reconfigurability. The paper argues you do not need maximum optical flexibility to capture much of the benefit.
  • Two-hop detouring is used as a traffic-engineering companion to optical topology changes, which keeps wavelength utilization bounded without assuming perfect direct circuits.
  • The intended timescale is seconds or longer, so this is not packet-level load balancing; it fits pod/rack-group demand shifts or scheduled collective phases.
  • TDMA transparency leaves a path for finer-grained sharing later without replacing the optical fabric.

Actionable lessons: When evaluating optical tiers for AI pods, model skew persistence and reconfiguration granularity explicitly. A partially reconfigurable fabric may be the sweet spot if demand is stable over seconds but not stable enough for a static topology.

verified arXiv paper · disaggregated memory, rdma/cxl, compiler-runtime systems

Decoupling Disaggregated Memory Optimizations from Indexing: A Compiler-Runtime Approach

Authors & affiliations: Xinpeng Zhao, Zeling Long, Chaichon Wongkham, Srijan Srivastava, and Eric Lo — The Chinese University of Hong Kong; Jiayi Liu — Purdue University; Baotong Lu — Microsoft Research; Tianzheng Wang — Simon Fraser University.

Tags: Disaggregated Memory, RDMA/CXL, Compiler-Runtime

Problem: Disaggregated memory puts data behind a slower remote interconnect, so indexes need caching, address translation, and pointer-dependency handling. Existing systems bake those optimizations into each index, making every data structure and platform a fresh redesign.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Presents Nox, a compiler-runtime framework that takes an unmodified concurrent index and generates a disaggregated-memory counterpart.
  • Uses LLVM IR rewriting to expose allocation, address, and pointer-dependency information to a centralized runtime for caching and translation.
  • Evaluates generated B+-tree, hash-table, and skip-list variants on real RDMA and CXL hardware, showing robust scaling and competitive performance versus specialized hand-crafted indexes.

Why it matters: The paper is database-flavored, but the infrastructure point is broader: remote-memory optimization should be a reusable runtime layer, not hand-spliced into each data structure.

Key insights:

  • Pointer-rich structures are a worst-case workload for disaggregated memory because each dependent access can become a remote round trip.
  • Compiler-extracted dependency information lets the runtime apply cache and translation policies without forcing application-level rewrites.
  • RDMA and CXL are treated as backends with different characteristics, which is exactly the portability problem heterogeneous memory fabrics create.
  • For AI serving, the analogous target is KV/prefix/expert metadata: avoid encoding fabric assumptions into every cache structure.

Actionable lessons: Design disaggregated AI memory systems with a narrow runtime contract for allocation, translation, caching, and dependency exposure. If every KV store, feature cache, or expert table directly owns RDMA/CXL tricks, portability will rot fast.

verified arXiv paper · deadline-aware network control, load balancing, rl training

From Prior-Guided Heuristics to Deployable Agents: Accelerating Demonstration-Driven Reinforcement Learning for Deadline-Constrained Network Control

Authors & affiliations: Vincenzo Norman Vitale, Mohammad Solki, and Antonia Maria Tulino — DIETI, University of Naples Federico II; Andreas F. Molisch — University of Southern California; Jaime Llorca — University of Trento, CTTC, and New York University. Antonia Maria Tulino is also affiliated with New York University.

Tags: Network Control, Deadline-Aware Routing, RL for Networking

Problem: Delay-sensitive flows need peak-latency guarantees in dynamic heterogeneous networks, but volume-based routing metrics ignore packet urgency and RL controllers trained from scratch are sample-inefficient and unstable early in training.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Defines Effective Congestion metrics that account for packet urgency and filter non-viable traffic before routing decisions.
  • Combines a distributed scheduler with a centralized RL router in MADRL EC, plus a Uniform Path Grouping heuristic for robust load balancing.
  • Introduces Model-Guided Annealed Reinforcement Learning, using demonstrations from a lightweight analytical heuristic to pretrain and guide online RL fine-tuning.

Why it matters: This is less AI-cluster-specific, but useful for thinking about deadline-aware transport/control loops: congestion should be measured against remaining slack, not just queue depth.

Key insights:

  • A queue is not equally congested for all packets; congestion depends on whether queued work still has enough lifetime to meet deadlines on candidate paths.
  • The analytical prior is queryable in online states, so imitation does not disappear after offline pretraining.
  • Hybrid control is pragmatic: keep local scheduling distributed while reserving global path selection for the learned controller.
  • For datacenter AI traffic, the comparable idea is slack-aware routing for collectives or inference stages with hard TTFT/iteration deadlines.

Actionable lessons: If a fabric carries latency-critical AI serving traffic, feed remaining-deadline/slack into load-balancing decisions. Queue occupancy alone can route packets onto paths that are “available” in bytes but already impossible in time.
