Most important paper of the day

3DLS: A 3D Logic-Stacked Architecture for Disaggregated LLM Serving

Authors & affiliations: Jaehun Lee, In-Jun Jung, and Joo-Young Kim. Affiliations: Graduate School of System Architect, KAIST; School of Electrical Engineering, KAIST.

Tags: Disaggregated LLM Serving, Scale-up Interconnects, KV Cache Transfer

Why this is the top pick: 3DLS attacks a very concrete serving-fabric problem: in PD-disaggregated LLM serving with tensor parallelism, layer-wise KV-cache transfer and decode-side AllReduce can fight over the same lateral die-to-die links. The paper’s answer is not just “more bandwidth”; it physically separates traffic classes by sending KV transfer vertically through a 3D-stacked path while leaving decode collectives on the lateral D2D fabric.

Deeper explanation: The useful mental model is that prefill/decode disaggregation creates two different network workloads on the package: bulky KV movement and latency-critical collectives. Priority scheduling on a shared fabric can reduce pain, but it cannot remove head-of-line interference when both traffic classes still consume the same physical links. 3DLS is interesting because it turns that into an architecture partitioning problem: isolate the traffic class that is not on the per-token collective critical path, then preserve the low-latency path for decode-side TP collectives.

  • Problem: Conventional 2D/2.5D chiplet serving designs force KV-cache transfer and decode AllReduce traffic onto the same lateral D2D interconnect, increasing token-generation latency under long-context PD serving.
  • Key contributions: Characterizes mixed KVT/AllReduce contention as a serving bottleneck; proposes a logic-on-logic 3D-stacked chiplet layout that routes KV transfer through vertical interconnects; reports up to 1.49× throughput and 60.2% lower E2E latency versus the shared-fabric planar baseline.
  • Why it matters architecturally: It argues for traffic-class isolation as a first-class scale-up fabric design principle for LLM serving, not just a scheduling policy layered on top of a shared link pool.

3DLS: A 3D Logic-Stacked Architecture for Disaggregated LLM Serving

Authors & affiliations: Jaehun Lee, In-Jun Jung, and Joo-Young Kim. Affiliations: Graduate School of System Architect, KAIST; School of Electrical Engineering, KAIST.

Tags: Disaggregated LLM Serving, Scale-up Interconnects, KV Cache Transfer

Problem: PD-disaggregated serving with tensor parallelism must move layer-wise KV blocks from prefill to decode while decode workers run latency-sensitive collectives. On planar chiplet fabrics, those traffic classes can share D2D links and create critical-path interference.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Characterizes KVT/AllReduce mixed-traffic contention in conventional 2D/2.5D chiplet-based PD-disaggregated serving.
  • Introduces 3DLS, a logic-on-logic 3D-stacked architecture that places prefill pools on a top tier and decode pools on a bottom tier, using vertical links for KV-cache transfer.
  • Shows up to 1.49× throughput and 60.2% lower E2E latency over a shared-fabric planar baseline, and up to 1.17× throughput and 31.4% lower latency over a priority-managed planar baseline.

Why it matters: This is directly relevant to scale-up AI fabrics because it separates bandwidth provisioning from critical-path isolation. A shared fabric with QoS is still shared; physical path separation can make decode collectives more predictable.

Key insights:

  • KV transfer and TP collectives have different urgency: KV movement is bulk state delivery, while decode AllReduce sits on the token step.
  • Priority management helps but cannot eliminate contention when both classes consume the same lateral links.
  • As TP degree and context length grow, the number of hops and KV volume amplify the penalty of shared-path designs.
  • 3D stacking is being used here less as a raw-bandwidth trick and more as a traffic-isolation mechanism.

Actionable lessons: When evaluating NVLink/NVL-style or chiplet-scale serving fabrics, model traffic classes separately: prefill-to-decode KV, decode collectives, and control traffic should not be collapsed into one average bandwidth demand. For architecture studies, compare shared-fabric QoS against physical separation; the latter may be required when collectives sit on per-token latency.

Verified arXiv CS paper · moe training / parallelism / communication-computation overlap

Mixture-of-Parallelisms: Towards Memory-Efficient Training Stack for Mixture-of-Experts Models

Authors & affiliations: Xuan-Phi Nguyen, Shrey Pandit, Yiran Zhao, Semih Yavuz, Silvio Savarese, and Shafiq Joty. Affiliations: Salesforce AI Research.

Tags: Distributed Training, MoE Systems, Parallelism Strategy

Problem: Trillion-parameter, million-token MoE training is bounded by a mix of GPU HBM, host DRAM, CPU-GPU bandwidth, GPU-GPU bandwidth, and node-node communication. A single global parallelism plan wastes headroom because attention, experts, projection, and optimizer state hit different bottlenecks.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Recasts long-context MoE training as a component-specialized sharding problem rather than a single Cartesian tensor/pipeline/data-parallel plan.
  • Combines memory-efficient expert parallelism, sharded data-tensor-parallel vocabulary projection, and an optimizer pipeline with computation-communication overlap.
  • Reports 4.7×–8.2× higher per-GPU throughput than a tuned FSDP2 baseline and supports contexts up to 1M tokens where the baseline runs out of memory beyond 64K–128K.

Why it matters: The networking hook is the resource-aware mapping: the paper explicitly treats node-node communication, GPU-GPU bandwidth, CPU-GPU movement, and host offload as separate constraints. That is the right level of thinking for heterogeneous AI superpods.

Key insights:

  • Different MoE components want different sharding axes; forcing one plan across all layers creates avoidable memory or communication peaks.
  • Overlapping rank sub-groups avoid the rank explosion of Cartesian 3D parallelism while still specializing communication patterns.
  • The optimizer step can be moved off the critical path only if host memory, CPU-GPU transfer, and communication overlap are designed together.
  • For small hardware budgets, memory efficiency can dominate raw bandwidth; the paper calls MoP the memory-efficiency extreme of the design space.

Actionable lessons: In training-stack design, do not benchmark “the parallelism strategy” as a monolith. Attribute bytes and memory peaks to attention, experts, vocab projection, gradients, and optimizer state separately, then assign sharding and overlap policy per component. For simulation, this argues for modeling memory hierarchy and interconnect tiers, not just all-reduce volume.

Verified arXiv CS paper · heterogeneous gpu clusters / llm serving / telemetry-aware placement

OmniPilot: An Uncertainty-Aware LLM Inference Advisor for Heterogeneous GPU Clusters

Authors & affiliations: D. Balamurugan and Thomas W. Bush. Affiliations: Kempner Institute for the Study of Natural and Artificial Intelligence, Harvard University.

Tags: Heterogeneous AI Infrastructure, LLM Serving, Cluster Telemetry

Problem: Operators on shared heterogeneous GPU clusters must choose GPU type, tensor-parallel degree, and precision before spending node-hours. Static recipes miss interactions among quantization, KV pressure, model family, launch failure rates, and fluctuating cluster state.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Builds a conformally calibrated quantile cost model with an out-of-distribution abstention layer for serving launch recommendations.
  • Ranks GPU × TP-degree × precision configurations with an economic utility metric that includes cost, throughput, failure risk, SLO risk, and KV-saturation penalties.
  • Evaluates across 460 benchmark runs on A100, H100, and H200 hardware; reports 6.2% MAPE for aggregate throughput and 95% top-1 placement accuracy on the frozen evaluation.

Why it matters: This is not a transport paper, but it is useful for cluster operations: tensor-parallel degree and KV saturation turn “which GPU should I use?” into an infrastructure decision that depends on memory, bandwidth, and failure priors.

Key insights:

  • Quantization is not monotonically better for throughput; hardware/runtime/model interactions can invert the expected ranking.
  • Launch success rates vary materially with TP degree, so placement should include failure probability, not just predicted tokens/s.
  • Scheduler metadata alone is insufficient for a throughput advisor because it records resource use, not workload semantics.
  • Abstention is a systems feature: outside the measured envelope, wrong confidence is worse than no recommendation.

Actionable lessons: For production inference placement, pair live scheduler state with workload-aware telemetry such as model family, precision, context length, concurrency, KV saturation, and measured success rates. Treat prediction intervals and OOD detection as part of the serving control plane; otherwise an advisor will silently overfit yesterday’s cluster.

Fast take

Read 3DLS first if you want the most network/fabric-relevant idea: isolate KV transfer from decode collectives instead of relying only on shared-fabric QoS. Then skim MoP for component-level parallelism assignment and OmniPilot for a telemetry/calibration view of heterogeneous serving placement.
