Today’s useful signal is mostly around heterogeneous inference boundaries: KV-cache handoff across dissimilar accelerators, mixed interconnect assumptions, and serving traces that expose where agent workloads actually stress prefill, cache, and tool-latency paths.

Most important paper of the day

HBM Is Not All You Need: Efficient Disaggregated LLM Serving across Memory-heterogeneous Accelerators

Authors & affiliations: Zhixiang Wei, Yun Wang, James Yen, Mingyuan Xia, and Zhengwei Qi. Affiliations: Shanghai Jiao Tong University; UltraRISC.

Tags: LLM Serving, Heterogeneous AI Infrastructure, KV Transfer

Why this is the top pick: HMA-Serve is directly about the cross-vendor prefill/decode split that turns KV movement from a local runtime detail into a systems boundary: format conversion, quantization policy, transfer path, and scheduling all become coupled.

Deeper explanation: The paper argues that HBM-heavy GPUs are a poor economic match for compute-bound prefill because most of their memory bandwidth sits idle, while decode is exactly where HBM matters. The proposed MemHA setup pairs cheaper GDDR-based accelerators for prefill with HBM GPUs for decode. That sounds simple until the two sides come from different vendors: the prefill worker may produce KV in a different precision, layout, and software stack than the decode GPU consumes. HMA-Serve’s key mechanism is to make that boundary explicit with phase-wise quantization, an on-GPU KV adapter, and a heterogeneity-aware scheduler.

  • Problem: Prefill/decode disaggregation can improve utilization, but the most cost-efficient prefill/decode hardware pair is often cross-vendor and memory-heterogeneous, breaking assumptions about native KV format and shared runtime support.
  • Key contributions: A MemHA serving model, the HMA-Serve system, phase-wise quantization for prefill/decode roles, an on-GPU KV adapter, and a scheduler that handles heterogeneous prefill/decode capacity.
  • Why it matters architecturally: The serving boundary is no longer just “send KV over the network.” It is a typed state-transfer boundary where representation, bandwidth, GPU copy/convert cost, and lifecycle policy decide whether disaggregation is actually cheaper.
  • Useful diagram: Figure 2 shows HMA-Serve’s architecture: a scheduler routes requests to Tenstorrent prefill workers and A100 decode workers, with KV conversion/transfer as the critical bridge.
Recent arXiv CS paper · memory-heterogeneous accelerators / disaggregated serving / KV transfer

HBM Is Not All You Need: Efficient Disaggregated LLM Serving across Memory-heterogeneous Accelerators

Authors & affiliations: Zhixiang Wei, Yun Wang, James Yen, Mingyuan Xia, and Zhengwei Qi. Affiliations: Shanghai Jiao Tong University; UltraRISC.

Tags: LLM Serving, Heterogeneous AI Infrastructure, KV Transfer

Problem: HBM-rich datacenter GPUs are expensive and underutilized during compute-bound prefill, while decode benefits strongly from HBM bandwidth. Splitting prefill and decode across GDDR and HBM accelerators is attractive, but cross-vendor KV handoff creates precision, layout, runtime, and scheduling problems.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Introduces Memory-Heterogeneous Accelerator serving, pairing GDDR-based accelerators for prefill with HBM-based GPUs for decode.
  • Presents HMA-Serve with phase-wise quantization, vendor-native low precision for prefill, BF16 decode, and an on-GPU KV adapter to bridge format differences.
  • Evaluates HMA-Serve against homogeneous disaggregation and heterogeneous colocation baselines, including goodput-per-dollar framing.

Why it matters: This is one of the more concrete ways to think about heterogeneous AI infra: not “which accelerator is fastest,” but which phase should run on which memory system and what the KV transfer boundary costs.

Key insights:

  • Prefill/decode disaggregation becomes much more interesting when the hardware is memory-heterogeneous rather than just separately allocated identical GPUs.
  • KV cache movement is both a network/data-plane issue and a semantic compatibility issue; raw bytes are not enough if dtype/layout assumptions diverge.
  • Phase-wise quantization is cleaner than one global precision policy because prefill and decode stress different resources.
  • The scheduler has to match request mix against asymmetric prefill/decode capacity; otherwise the cheaper prefill tier can just move the bottleneck.
  • The goodput-per-budget view is useful for cluster design because it exposes when expensive HBM should be reserved for memory-bound decode.

Actionable lessons: For heterogeneous serving designs, define the KV state contract explicitly: dtype, layout, ownership, conversion site, and failure cleanup. Model KV conversion as part of the data path, not a free library call. For architecture studies, compare cost-normalized phase capacity rather than peak accelerator FLOPS.

Useful diagram: Figure 2 is the architecture diagram to inspect; it shows the scheduler, Tenstorrent prefill workers, A100 decode workers, and the KV adapter/transfer path. Figure 1 is also useful for the accelerator roofline argument.

Recent arXiv CS paper · heterogeneous PD inference / KV state / interconnect boundaries

Demystifying the Design Space and Best Practices for Heterogeneous LLM Inference and Serving

Authors & affiliations: Zhixin Wang, Zhengbo Wang, Fangcheng Fu, Yinhui Lu, Jinlong Hou, Yijie Chen, Xiaowei Shen, He Liu, Xiangbin Li, Jun Chen, Ruya Gu, Dian Wang, Zhou Tan, Yuan Cheng, Hongzhou Zhang, Xiangjun Huang, Ping Zhang, and Xiaohe Hu. Affiliations: Shanghai Innovation Institute; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Fudan University; Renmin University of China; MetaX Integrated Circuits (Shanghai) Co., Ltd.; Infrawaves; Shanghai AI Power Technology Co., Ltd.

Tags: LLM Serving, Heterogeneous Infrastructure, Interconnect & KV Residency

Problem: Heterogeneous prefill-decode serving mixes accelerator types, precisions, interconnects, and KV residency tiers. The hard part is knowing which choices can be tuned locally and which become coupled at the PD boundary.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Organizes heterogeneous PD inference around accelerator, precision, interconnect, KV residency, and workload-induced stage pressure.
  • Identifies three recurring boundary decisions: compute placement, KV representation, and KV ownership.
  • Provides best-practice guidance for runtime role-specific precision, KV state portability, and handoff lifecycle management.

Why it matters: This paper is a useful design checklist for the same class of system as HMA-Serve. It says the PD boundary must carry semantics and lifecycle, not just bytes over an interconnect.

Key insights:

  • KV transfer engines often move buffers, not tensor semantics, so format mismatch can become a silent correctness or performance bug.
  • Precision policy belongs to runtime roles: prefill and decode may want different formats even within one request lifecycle.
  • KV ownership is a lifecycle protocol: reserve destination capacity, commit handoff, release source state, and recover from failures.
  • Interconnect and cross-vendor constraints are still partly open; the paper frames them as deployment-grounded guidance rather than fully benchmarked claims.
  • The coupling matrix is a good mental model for reviewing whether a proposed serving optimization composes with heterogeneous hardware.

Actionable lessons: In serving architecture reviews, add an explicit “PD boundary contract” section: representation compatibility, ownership transition, transient capacity, transfer engine behavior, and fallback path. For simulations, avoid modeling KV transfer as just bandwidth × bytes; include conversion and residency decisions.

Useful diagram: Figure 1 gives a five-axis view around runtime KV state; Figure 2 is the coupling matrix over design axes; Figures 5 and 6 are particularly useful for KV portability and handoff lifecycle.

Recent arXiv CS paper · agent workloads / serving traces / KV-cache behavior

TraceLab: Characterizing Coding Agent Workloads for LLM Serving

Authors & affiliations: Kan Zhu, Mathew Jacob, Chenxi Ma, Yi Pan, Stephanie Wang, Arvind Krishnamurthy, and Baris Kasikci. Affiliations: University of Washington; Wuhan University of Technology; Shanghai Jiao Tong University.

Tags: LLM Serving, Workload Tracing, KV Cache

Problem: Coding agents are becoming a major LLM serving workload, but public benchmarks do not capture long autonomous loops, tool calls, context growth, prefix-cache reuse, and human-paced gaps in real sessions.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Collects and releases a trace of roughly 4,300 coding-agent sessions with about 350,000 LLM steps and 430,000 tool calls from Claude Code and Codex usage.
  • Characterizes long contexts with short outputs, heavy-tailed tool latency, multi-step autonomous loops, and high but imperfect prefix-cache hit rates.
  • Identifies serving opportunities including lower-overhead tool calling, append-length-aware prefill, semantic-aware tool-latency prediction, and better KV-cache management around idle gaps.

Why it matters: This is not a fabric paper, but it gives workload evidence for why agent serving stresses prefill, KV residency, and cache lifecycle differently from simple chat. That matters for disaggregated serving and memory/network provisioning.

Key insights:

  • Agent sessions are multi-step workflows, so prefix-cache lifecycle and append-token behavior become first-order serving concerns.
  • Long-append steps are rare but contribute a large share of prefill work, which can create bursty pressure on prefill resources.
  • Tool-call latency has a heavy tail, creating windows where KV retention, eviction, or migration policy can matter.
  • Provider behavior differs enough that a serving system should not assume one universal agent trace shape.
  • The trace is useful input for future network/KV disaggregation studies because it exposes when state is reused, extended, or idle.

Actionable lessons: For agent-serving experiments, drive the system with multi-step traces rather than independent requests. Track append length, prefix-cache hit rate, tool gaps, and KV retention time. If prefill and decode are disaggregated, use agent traces to test whether KV ownership and eviction policies survive long autonomous loops.

Useful diagram: Figures 1 and 2 show prefix versus append-token behavior; Figure 5 explains output accounting schemes; Figures 7–9 summarize tool-call count and latency distributions.

Recent arXiv CS paper · sequence parallelism / NCCL-level communication / long-context training

HSAP: A Hierachical Sequence-aware Parallelism for Hybrid-Context Generative Models

Authors & affiliations: Songxin Zhang, Zejian Xie, Zhuoyang Song, Cong lin, Junyu Lu, Jiaxing Zhang, and Bingyi Jing. Affiliations: Department of Statistics and Data Science, Southern University of Science and Technology; International Digital Economy Academy.

Tags: Collective Communication, Long-Context Training, Parallelism

Problem: Sequence packing reduces padding waste for long-context generative models, but existing sequence-parallel attention schemes struggle with hybrid-context packed sequences, cross-contamination, and communication overhead across device groups.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Proposes a sequence-aware parallelism algorithm for hybrid-context packed sequences.
  • Uses just-in-time compilation to optimize communication strategy across device groups at the NCCL level.
  • Combines Ulysses-style and Ring-Attention-style ideas into a hierarchical sequence-aware parallelism framework with explicit memory and communication management.

Why it matters: The networking relevance is narrower but real: long-context training increasingly depends on sequence-parallel communication patterns, and correctness constraints from packed attention can force different all-to-all/P2P schedules.

Key insights:

  • Packed-sequence correctness can constrain the communication schedule, not just the attention mask.
  • All-to-all and ring-style P2P are complementary; a hierarchy can use each where it fits the attention dependency structure.
  • JIT-compiled communication plans are a useful direction when static collectives over-communicate for irregular packed contexts.
  • The paper’s “hierachical network hardware topology” figure makes the topology assumption explicit, which is good for performance interpretation.

Actionable lessons: For long-context training systems, benchmark sequence packing together with the exact collective/P2P plan; do not treat packing as only a compute-side optimization. In simulator work, model attention-dependency sparsity and communicator hierarchy, otherwise sequence-parallel results can be misleading.

Useful diagram: Figure 2 shows the JIT compile-execute architecture; Figure 3 shows the compilation algorithm for communication strategies; Figure 4 shows the hierarchical network hardware topology assumed by HSAP.

Recent arXiv CS paper · optical WAN / geo-distributed AI / CCL over wide-area links

LLMs and Optical Networks: A Symbiotic Relationship

Authors & affiliations: Mëmëdhe Ibrahimi, Qiaolun Zhang, Giovanni S. Sticca, Jiaheng Xiong, Francesco Musumeci, and Massimo Tornatore. Affiliations: Politecnico di Milano.

Tags: Optical Networking, Geo-distributed Training, AI Clusters

Problem: Very large AI training may outgrow single-datacenter power, capacity, and data-locality constraints, pushing training toward geographically distributed clusters where optical WAN latency and path heterogeneity become training bottlenecks.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Frames a two-way relationship: LLMs need optical transport for geo-distributed training, and LLMs can help automate optical network management.
  • Highlights WAN-aware collective communication, ZR/ZR+ pluggables, and hollow-core fiber as technical enablers for inter-DC AI training.
  • Discusses LLM-assisted intent-driven networking, failure management, orchestration, planning, and optimization for optical networks.

Why it matters: This is a short position-style paper, but the “scale-across” angle is in Ethan’s lane: once training crosses datacenters, CCL design and optical transport become coupled rather than independent layers.

Key insights:

  • Geo-distributed training is straggler-sensitive; the slowest inter-site path can dominate iteration time.
  • WAN-aware CCLs need to reason about asymmetric links and partial control, especially outside private hyperscaler backbones.
  • Hollow-core fiber and coherent pluggables are presented as physical-layer levers for latency and capacity, not just generic bandwidth upgrades.
  • The paper usefully separates AI-for-network-management from network-for-AI-training, which are often blurred.

Actionable lessons: For scale-across studies, model optical WAN topology, latency asymmetry, and failure domains directly in the collective scheduler. Treat inter-DC CCL policy as a cross-layer problem: logical tensor scheduling plus physical optical path constraints.

Useful diagram: Figure 1 gives a system overview linking LLM training sites, collective communication, and optical WANs; Figure 2 illustrates logical topology reconfiguration and WAN adaptation; Figure 3 sketches SSMF/HCF inter-DC connections.
