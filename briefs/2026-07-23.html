MoX: Efficient MoE Routing on Direct-Connect Topologies

Authors & affiliations: Ori Cohen, Jakob Krebs, Daniel Amir, and Mark Silberstein. Affiliations: Technion; NVIDIA.

Tags: MoE Communication, Direct-Connect Fabrics, Optical/Scale-out Networking

Why this is the top pick: This is the cleanest network paper in today’s batch: it asks whether MoE can run efficiently on static low-degree direct-connect/OCS-style fabrics without falling back to demand-driven circuit reconfiguration. That maps directly onto the superpod question of whether routing semantics can rescue sparse expert traffic before you add more packet switching.

Deeper explanation: MoX reframes expert-parallel traffic as a routing problem with semantics. Instead of assuming sparse MoE traffic requires a dynamically reconfigured optical fabric, it builds static multicast/reduction trees and balances relay load with offline link weights. The important systems idea is that the network does not need to know the exact future traffic matrix if the routing policy understands the top-K expert selection structure and the physical topology. For AI superpods, that is a strong hint that fabric efficiency can come from routing/dataflow co-design, not only from adding bandwidth or faster circuit switching.

  • Problem: Optical and direct-connect fabrics work well for regular dense-model collectives, but MoE dispatch/combine traffic is sparse, token-dependent, and skewed, making static topology design look brittle.
  • Key contributions: Constructs token-aware multicast trees for MoE dispatch and reverses them for combine with partial reduction at intermediate accelerators.; Uses offline-computed, top-K-specific link weights to balance relay traffic without predicting each batch traffic matrix or reconfiguring the fabric at runtime.; Evaluates recorded DeepSeek-V3 and Qwen-3 MoE traces in ASTRA-sim; reports up to 1.8× full MoE-block speedup over min-hop routing and up to 47% lower dispatch bottleneck link load on a 1,024-TPU Boardfly model.
  • Why it matters architecturally: MoE is exactly where dense collective assumptions break. MoX gives a concrete way to reason about sparse expert traffic on static low-degree fabrics.

MoX: Efficient MoE Routing on Direct-Connect Topologies

Authors & affiliations: Ori Cohen, Jakob Krebs, Daniel Amir, and Mark Silberstein. Affiliations: Technion; NVIDIA.

Tags: MoE Communication, Direct-Connect Fabrics, Optical/Scale-out Networking

Problem: Optical and direct-connect fabrics work well for regular dense-model collectives, but MoE dispatch/combine traffic is sparse, token-dependent, and skewed, making static topology design look brittle.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Constructs token-aware multicast trees for MoE dispatch and reverses them for combine with partial reduction at intermediate accelerators.
  • Uses offline-computed, top-K-specific link weights to balance relay traffic without predicting each batch traffic matrix or reconfiguring the fabric at runtime.
  • Evaluates recorded DeepSeek-V3 and Qwen-3 MoE traces in ASTRA-sim; reports up to 1.8× full MoE-block speedup over min-hop routing and up to 47% lower dispatch bottleneck link load on a 1,024-TPU Boardfly model.

Why it matters: This is the cleanest network paper in today’s batch: it asks whether MoE can run efficiently on static low-degree direct-connect/OCS-style fabrics without falling back to demand-driven circuit reconfiguration. That maps directly onto the superpod question of whether routing semantics can rescue sparse expert traffic before you add more packet switching.

Key insights:

  • MoE dispatch is better modeled as per-token multicast than as independent unicasts; exploiting the shared token payload reduces bandwidth tax.
  • Static routing is not automatically bad for MoE if it is optimized against the topology and expert-selection semantics rather than min-hop shortest paths.
  • Random expander-style low-radius topologies reduce hop count but can create relay hotspots; MoX’s link weights are a load-balancing layer over that structural asymmetry.
  • The Boardfly result is useful because it shows endpoint/cabling asymmetry can dominate even in a nominally regular production-inspired topology.

Actionable lessons: For expert-parallel fabrics, expose token-to-expert semantics to the routing layer. Benchmark multicast-aware routing against min-hop and ideal-switch baselines using recorded MoE token traces, and track maximum link load as a first-order stall predictor for dispatch/combine phases.

Fine-grained Computation-Communication Overlap via Tile-level Signaling and Scheduling for Mixture-of-Experts

Authors & affiliations: Minyu Cui, Anna Wingkvist, and Morgan Ericsson. Affiliations: Linnaeus University, Växjö, Sweden.

Tags: MoE Systems, Communication/Computation Overlap, GPU Runtime

Problem: Distributed MoE layers pay two all-to-all communications; the second all-to-all often waits until expert compute finishes, exposing communication latency on the critical path.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Introduces a producer-consumer design where a persistent compute kernel signals tile completion and a persistent communication kernel transfers ready row bands.
  • Uses remote-owner-aligned row layout so the communication kernel can issue contiguous remote writes without per-row routing logic.
  • Evaluates on a 4×A100 platform across three MoE models and reports up to 2.64× end-to-end speedup and 2.74× MoE-layer speedup over baselines.

Why it matters: The fabric lesson is that “overlap communication” needs to be aligned with GEMM tile structure and ownership layout, not just chunked collectives in separate streams. This is a runtime/data-layout paper, but it directly attacks exposed all-to-all latency in MoE execution.

Key insights:

  • Chunking at a tensor dimension can preserve API simplicity but often misses tile-level readiness, leaving bandwidth idle.
  • Separating persistent producer and consumer kernels avoids some intrusive fusion complexity while keeping synchronization device-resident.
  • Dedicated SM partitions turn communication progress into an explicit GPU scheduling resource; that knob matters under contention.
  • The method targets forward-pass MoE inference; backward/training overlap remains a natural next stress test.

Actionable lessons: Design MoE layouts so communication granularity follows ownership and tile readiness. When evaluating overlap, report both operator-level and full-layer speedups, and sweep SM partitions because the “communication engine” consumes scarce GPU execution resources.

A Training-Memory Regression in MLA Sequence Parallelism: Why Megatron-Core Forbids Absorption, and LAGA -- a Communication-Efficient Fix

Authors & affiliations: Changzheng Ma. Affiliations: China Mobile Jiutian Artificial Intelligence Technology (Beijing) Co., Ltd., Beijing, China.

Tags: Sequence Parallelism, Collectives, LLM Training

Problem: Megatron-Core has a low-communication absorbed MLA path for inference, but forbids it during training; the paper investigates whether that guard hides a real memory/communication trade-off.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Quantifies why absorbed MLA is a training memory trap: ported to training, it inflates activation memory by 20–34%, up to 9.2 GB at DeepSeek-V3 scale and 19.2 GB under a fused kernel.
  • Proposes LAGA, which gathers compressed MLA latents and reconstructs per-head K/V locally, preserving explicit-form memory while reducing collective traffic.
  • Validates at DeepSeek-V3 dimensions on 8×Ascend 910B; reports 1.98× lower collective communication and 1.07–1.24× cross-node attention-block throughput improvement with fused attention.

Why it matters: This is useful for Ethan because it is a concrete example of communication reduction colliding with activation memory and kernel implementation. The interesting part is not just “less all-gather”; it is the shape-level reason a serving optimization fails when moved into long-context training.

Key insights:

  • The compressed latent is a good communication object, but absorbing it into training attention creates large nh × dkv intermediates.
  • Sequence-parallel MLA is structurally awkward because Ulysses-style head sharding assumes per-head K/V shards, while MLA has a shared latent.
  • The best fix keeps the low-byte collective but rejects the inference-side algebraic reformulation.
  • Cross-node wins strengthen at the regime where long-context MLA training is most communication-bound.

Actionable lessons: Before porting inference-side memory tricks into training, audit live activation shapes and not just transfer bytes. For MLA sequence parallelism, separate “what crosses the wire” from “what tensor shape the attention kernel materializes.”

Useful diagram: Figure 2 is the key dataflow diagram comparing explicit, absorbed, and LAGA paths. Figure 1 also concisely contrasts communication reduction against the absorbed-form memory regression.

SLAI T-Rex: Full-Parameter Post-training of the DeepSeek-V4 Family on Ascend SuperPOD

Authors & affiliations: Dongfang Li, Xiaodong Luo, Ruoyu Sun, Xuhui Chen, Linyuan Qiu, Jian Meng, Zhengxuan Lu, Yiting Wang, Yucheng Xie, Tao Guo, Tianxiang Fang, Jing Li, Sihang Chen, Shihao Hong, Chang Liu, Weihua Dai, Zirong Zeng, Ziwei Zhu, Zhuohan Wang, Zhengjun Yue, Igor Vasilyev, Min Liu, Weijian Sun, Xin Chen, Yingmeng Gao, Jinhua Zhou, Taolue Chen, Chenwei Wu, Dong Zhang, Wenlong Jin, Jinmin Xiang, Barkova Maria, Ushakov Anton, Xianfei Jin, Tian Ding, Zhihang Lin, Qian Chen, Linxin Yang, Mingzhe Yang, Bingwei Zhang, Hongzhang Yang, Fangxue Zhang, Shijun Qin, Jie Yu, Cuihua Hu, Tolstykh Vasiliy, Nosov Ivan, Abdullin Amir, Zhichen Zhou, Xin Zhang, Zhixiong Ning, Xutong Zhao, Junjie Huang, Jiajun Liu, Weiyan Kong, Zheng Zhang, Wenhan Luo, Lin Hu, Yangbo Guo, Li Zeng, Shihao Zeng, Baotian Hu, Min Zhang, Haizhou Li, and Zhiquan Luo. Affiliations: AI Training Platform Team, Shenzhen Loop Area Institute; the title page lists Shenzhen Loop Area Institute.

Tags: Ascend SuperPOD, Heterogeneous AI Infrastructure, Training Systems

Problem: Full-parameter post-training of trillion-parameter MoE models on Ascend NPU infrastructure faces memory pressure, non-overlapped communication, and kernel inefficiency compared with GPU-centric recipes.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Presents a hierarchical optimization framework spanning model parallelism, computation-communication orchestration, and low-level Ascend kernel execution.
  • Reports DeepSeek-V4-Pro training MFU improvement from 11.67% to 34.22%, a 2.93× improvement over the open-source baseline recipe.
  • Builds an OR-focused CPT/SFT workflow on top of the optimized infrastructure and reports stronger zero-shot OR modeling scores for the specialized model.

Why it matters: This is less pure networking than MoX, but it is a real superpod systems report on non-GPU accelerators. The useful signal is the set of bottlenecks: dispatch latency bubbles, NPU-CPU co-optimization, and Ascend-specific kernel/parallelism tuning.

Key insights:

  • The paper frames communication orchestration as one layer in a full-stack optimization stack, not an isolated NCCL/HCCL knob.
  • Figure 2’s latency breakdown highlights that trillion-parameter MoE training bottlenecks are distributed across communication bubbles, memory management, and small-kernel tails.
  • ETP dispatcher scheduling and compute-communication overlap are treated as system-level levers for Ascend, which is relevant to heterogeneous NPU/GPU fleet design.
  • MFU gains come from accumulated cross-layer fixes; no single optimization is likely to transfer alone.

Actionable lessons: When evaluating heterogeneous accelerator superpods, build a bottleneck anatomy first: parallelism layout, dispatcher scheduling, host/device memory path, and kernel mix. Compare against open recipes, but expect vendor-specific collectives and kernel behavior to dominate portability.

Robust KV Cache Management for LLM Serving under Output Token Length Uncertainty

Authors & affiliations: Jiaming Cheng, Duong The Do, and Duong Tung Nguyen. Affiliations: Arizona State University, Tempe, AZ, USA.

Tags: LLM Serving, KV Cache, Robust Resource Control

Problem: Serving systems must reserve KV cache before knowing output length; reserving too little causes preemption and recomputation, while reserving too much wastes HBM and reduces goodput.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Formulates joint optimization over GPU parallelism configuration, per-class KV reservation, request routing across heterogeneous serving groups, and prefix caching.
  • Uses a Wasserstein distributionally robust optimization formulation and derives a critical-fractile reservation structure that adapts to preemption versus memory-waste costs.
  • Evaluates on BurstGPT, Azure, and ShareGPT traces; reports up to 56% lower cost than fixed-quantile reservation baselines while maintaining competitive P99 latency, goodput, and SLO violation rates.

Why it matters: This is more control-plane than fabric, but it touches the serving-memory/network boundary: routing requests across heterogeneous groups and P/D-style data planes only works if KV residency is robust under drift. It is a useful counterpart to transport-level KV transfer papers.

Key insights:

  • Fixed P90/P95/P99 reservation is fragile because output-length tails and cost regimes shift by workload class.
  • The reservation policy couples to routing and parallelism configuration; treating memory reservation as a local admission heuristic misses cluster-level trade-offs.
  • Rolling-horizon re-optimization is important because prompt templates, user behavior, and traffic mix drift.
  • The framework leaves the data plane unchanged, which makes it more deployable but also means it depends on accurate telemetry and cost modeling.

Actionable lessons: Expose KV reservation as a control-plane variable alongside routing and parallelism. Track preemption cost, wasted HBM, P99 latency, and goodput under distribution shift; do not hard-code one global output-length quantile for all request classes.

Fast take

Read MoX first if you care about fabric design for expert-parallel traffic. Then skim the tile-level overlap paper for GPU-runtime mechanics, LAGA for the communication-vs-activation-memory trade-off in MLA training, and SLAI T-Rex for a heterogeneous SuperPOD bottleneck anatomy. The KV-cache paper is the serving control-plane pick.
