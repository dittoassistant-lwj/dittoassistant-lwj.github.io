AI cluster networking & infrastructure brief

Today’s batch is mostly about moving state across awkward boundaries: KV cache over wide-area Ethernet, decode operators across heterogeneous GPU groups, HBM pages between experts and KV cache, and topology truth in programmable networks.

Most important paper of the day

PDD: Unleashing Economical and Flexible Heterogeneous LLM Inference via Cross-Datacenter Prefill-Decode Disaggregation

Authors & affiliations: Yida Wang, Xiuhong Li, Jianping Ma, Gan Sun, Yunshen Xu, Buhe Han, Jingxu Ng, Yuhao Luo, Ke Hong, Guohao Dai, Boxun Li, and Yu Wang — Infinigence-AI; Tsinghua University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Peking University.

Tags: Cross-DC LLM Serving, KV Cache Transfer, RDMA/Ethernet

Why this is the top pick: This is the clearest network-facing paper today. It treats disaggregated LLM inference as a real data-movement problem: prefill and decode may live in different geographically separated clusters, but KV cache movement over cheap wide-area Ethernet can dominate TTFT unless the serving architecture explicitly masks it.

Deeper explanation: PDD adds a Relay Decode instance near the prefill cluster. Prefill sends KV cache quickly to the relay over RDMA while also sending it slowly to the main decode cluster over TCP/Ethernet. The relay starts emitting early tokens while the wide-area KV transfer completes; then the main decode side uses an extend-decode handoff, transferring only token IDs and recomputing incremental KV locally rather than shipping more bulky KV state. The architectural point is strong: when cross-cluster bandwidth is 10–100 Gbps but aggregate KV demand can exceed 180 Gbps, latency hiding and cache-aware routing become first-class serving primitives, not optional runtime polish.

  • Problem: Cross-datacenter prefill/decode disaggregation enables flexible heterogeneous deployment, but wide-area Ethernet KV transfer creates multi-second TTFT tails, especially for agentic workloads with long contexts and short outputs.
  • Key contributions:
  • Proposes a three-tier Prefill–Relay Decode–Main Decode architecture that overlaps RDMA-local relay decoding with slow cross-DC TCP/Ethernet KV transfer.
  • Introduces Decode-side RadixCache, extend-decode handoff, and multi-stage pipeline orchestration to reduce bandwidth pressure and keep relay/main decode state consistent.
  • Reports about 46% lower P90 TTFT versus a cross-datacenter PD baseline and up to 37.5% higher benefit-cost ratio versus an intra-DC homogeneous PD baseline in SLA-compliant goodput.
  • Why it matters architecturally: PDD is a reminder that KV cache is becoming a network payload with transport-level consequences; serving design has to reason about bandwidth, tail latency, cache hit rate, and handoff semantics together.
  • Useful diagram: Figure 3 is the architecture diagram: P and RLD sit in one RDMA domain while MD sits across wide-area Ethernet. Figure 4 is the useful timing diagram showing how relay decoding masks Ethernet KV transfer before the handoff.

PDD: Unleashing Economical and Flexible Heterogeneous LLM Inference via Cross-Datacenter Prefill-Decode Disaggregation

Authors & affiliations: Yida Wang, Xiuhong Li, Jianping Ma, Gan Sun, Yunshen Xu, Buhe Han, Jingxu Ng, Yuhao Luo, Ke Hong, Guohao Dai, Boxun Li, and Yu Wang — Infinigence-AI; Tsinghua University; Shanghai Jiao Tong University; Peking University.

Tags: Cross-DC LLM Serving, KV Cache Transfer, RDMA/Ethernet

Problem: Cross-datacenter prefill/decode disaggregation can reuse existing heterogeneous clusters, but KV cache transfer over low-cost wide-area Ethernet creates bandwidth pressure and TTFT tails that are especially painful for long-context agentic workloads.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Designs a Prefill–Relay Decode–Main Decode architecture where the relay receives KV over local RDMA and begins decoding while the main decode cluster waits for TCP/Ethernet KV transfer.
  • Uses Decode-side RadixCache to reduce cross-DC KV bandwidth and extend-decode handoff to move only token IDs while recomputing incremental KV on the main decode side.
  • Evaluates DeepSeek-V4-pro on real agentic traces, reporting roughly 46% lower P90 TTFT than a cross-DC PD baseline and up to 37.5% higher benefit-cost ratio than an intra-DC homogeneous PD baseline.

Why it matters: This directly connects LLM serving architecture to datacenter-network realities: KV cache is large, bursty state, and the network path determines whether disaggregation is economical or just a TTFT tax generator.

Key insights:

  • Cross-DC KV transfer can account for 43–56% of end-to-end latency for short-output agentic requests, so hiding transfer latency can matter more than optimizing decode throughput alone.
  • The relay is not a full duplicate decode pool; its job is to cover the Ethernet transfer window for the minority of low-hit-rate/high-payload requests.
  • Sending token IDs and locally recomputing incremental KV is often cheaper and more stable than transferring additional KV state across the WAN path.
  • Cache consistency is a scaling constraint: routing low-hit-rate requests only through the relay can starve the main decode cache and eventually overload the relay path.

Actionable lessons: For cross-cluster serving, model KV transfer as a tail-latency-critical transport workload. Track prefix hit rate, KV payload size, RDMA versus TCP transfer time, and handoff cost together; then decide whether to add relay capacity, cache admission, or topology-aware routing.

OpWeave: Flexible Operator Disaggregation for Heterogeneous LLM Serving

Authors & affiliations: Zikun Li, Yixuan Mei, Shiqi Pan, Zixuan Chen, Xiaowen Zhang, Mengdi Wu, Shuhuai Lin, Yutong Yang, Zhihao Zhang, and Zhihao Jia — Carnegie Mellon University; Xupeng Miao — Peking University; Rashmi Vinayak — Carnegie Mellon University and Google.

Tags: Heterogeneous LLM Serving, Operator Disaggregation, Pipeline Runtime

Problem: Existing operator-level disaggregated serving systems often fix the split at attention–FFN or attention–MoE boundaries, which can over-constrain stage balance, hardware assignment, and activation movement on heterogeneous GPU clusters.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Introduces an analytical cost model that bounds when homogeneous and heterogeneous operator-level disaggregation should beat colocated serving.
  • Formulates planning across operator partitioning, hardware assignment, parallelism, batching, and pipeline orchestration, using partition blocks to keep the search tractable.
  • Builds a vLLM-based runtime for flexible operator stages; reports up to 1.78× lower serving cost on homogeneous H100 clusters and up to 1.89× lower cost on heterogeneous clusters versus the best feasible baselines.

Why it matters: This is a systems-boundary paper: the “right” disaggregation unit is no longer just prefill/decode or attention/FFN. It depends on operator resource shape, KV capacity coupling, communication exposure, and available accelerator mix.

Key insights:

  • Decode attention and FFN/MoE stress different resources; attention can be memory/KV limited while FFN/MoE wants high arithmetic throughput and larger batch sizes.
  • Fixed attention–FFN disaggregation can create too many pipeline stages and too much inter-node activation transfer; OpWeave reports up to 22.8× less inter-node data transfer per output token than AFD in one comparison.
  • Planner regularity matters because modern hybrid-attention models vary by layer; a per-layer brute-force search is not a practical deployment mechanism.
  • The runtime side is as important as the cost model: one native call per decoding iteration and staged communication are needed to avoid orchestration overhead eating the theoretical gain.

Actionable lessons: Build serving planners around resource vectors per operator — HBM capacity, memory bandwidth, tensor throughput, activation bytes, and stage-balance constraints — rather than hard-coded serving phases. Treat activation movement as a first-class network cost even inside a “GPU serving” paper.

Dynamic HBM Repartitioning for Multi-Turn MoE Serving

Authors & affiliations: Jinpyo Kim and Mingi Kwon — UC San Diego; Younghoon Min and Jongryool Kim — SK hynix; Jishen Zhao — UC San Diego.

Tags: MoE Serving, HBM Management, KV Cache

Problem: Multi-turn agentic workloads accumulate reusable KV state, while MoE expert weights occupy most HBM even though only a sparse subset is active per token. Static HBM partitioning forces early KV eviction, repeated prefill, and request preemption.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Proposes VAMP, a vLLM-based serving framework that dynamically shifts HBM pages between expert weights and the KV-cache pool.
  • Uses a three-way cost model comparing expert offload, KV-cache eviction, and request preemption, then remaps pages with CUDA Virtual Memory Management without copying resident KV data.
  • Reports that on a 2,103-turn SWE-bench agent replay, VAMP with 15% max expert offload reduces TTFT p90 from 26.1 s to 1.10 s and increases throughput by 20.7%, with a 31.1% TPOT increase.

Why it matters: This is not a network paper in the narrow sense, but it is very relevant to heterogeneous AI infrastructure: it treats accelerator memory as a movable boundary between state residency and data movement, which is the same trade-off that shows up across CXL, host memory, SSD, and fabric-backed KV designs.

Key insights:

  • The “prefix-cache cliff” is threshold-driven: once reusable context exceeds the fixed KV pool, hit rate collapses and TTFT jumps sharply rather than degrading smoothly.
  • MoE creates reclaimable HBM because many expert weights are resident but inactive for any given token; the trick is knowing when offloading experts is cheaper than evicting KV.
  • CUDA VMM page remapping matters because copying resident KV during repartitioning would add exactly the kind of latency bubble the system is trying to avoid.
  • The trade-off is explicit: VAMP buys lower TTFT and higher throughput at the cost of higher TPOT from expert transfers.

Actionable lessons: In MoE serving, do not reserve static HBM pools as if weight residency and KV residency are independent. Add telemetry for prefix-hit-rate collapse, preemptions, expert-transfer exposure, and KV pool saturation; use these as inputs to dynamic memory/fabric-tier policies.

Rethinking Software-Defined Networking Link Discovery with Dynamic Randomization

Authors & affiliations: Mingming Chen — University of Texas at Dallas; Teryl Taylor and Frederico Araujo — IBM Research; Benjamin E. Ujcich — Georgetown University; Thomas La Porta — The Pennsylvania State University; Trent Jaeger — University of California, Riverside.

Tags: SDN Reliability, Topology Discovery, Network Security

Problem: SDN controllers rely on topology discovery to build the ground-truth network graph, but LLDP-based discovery packets expose static identifiers that attackers can exploit to fabricate links or poison topology state.

Key contributions from the authors:

  • Analyzes five open-source SDN controllers and ten discovery protocols, finding that all remain vulnerable to at least one form of link-fabrication attack.
  • Introduces ChameleonDisc, a dynamic discovery protocol using DecoyType, MorphType, and CamoType probes to remove targetable static LLDP signatures and detect manipulation.
  • Implements the design in OpenDaylight; on a 252-link topology, reports 1.37–4.53 s median convergence for legitimate link changes and 4.06 s median relay detection, with 13 percentage points of extra mean CPU.

Why it matters: For AI fabrics, topology truth is operationally sacred. Even if this paper is not AI-specific, poisoned topology state is exactly the kind of control-plane corruption that can break traffic engineering, failover, or isolation assumptions in large programmable fabrics.

Key insights:

  • The root problem is not just missing cryptography: relay and flow-entry-induced attacks can preserve packet contents while misleading the controller about link destination.
  • Randomized MorphType packets remove stable LLDP signatures, while CamoType packets blend with ordinary traffic to force attackers into a harder detection dilemma.
  • The design has a clear security–performance knob: longer discovery intervals reduce CPU and state overhead but increase detection latency.
  • Compatibility with existing SDN architectures is a practical strength; the paper explicitly avoids requiring switch modifications.

Actionable lessons: Treat topology discovery as part of fabric reliability, not just controller plumbing. For programmable AI cluster networks, validate whether discovery/control-plane packets expose stable signatures and whether failover logic can tolerate adversarial or faulty topology updates.
